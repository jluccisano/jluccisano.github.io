    Version française

    I am a kind of hybrid. I can work as a developer, architect, DevOps engineer, SRE or full stack engineer, and I do not really have one specific hat. I do not consider myself an expert in every field, framework or language I have explored. My job is to find technical solutions to a functional need. An engineer does not need to know everything. What matters is knowing how to find the right information to solve a problem or add a piece to the puzzle.

    Since 2023, I have been a Senior Principal Engineer at Semtech, where I design and operate a cloud-native, event-driven platform and take care of site reliability (SRE), which fits my philosophy. No two days are the same. I learn something new every day, which is why my work is my passion.

    Cloud computing and SaaS platforms have made software more complex than ever. We are far from the CD-ROM era and the V model. Today we talk about scalability, high availability, big data and containers, in an agile model. We need to develop, deploy and test fixes and new features, but we also need to scale and migrate quickly while keeping the service highly available. Teams with a single narrow role would take too long to do this. So we split the service into micro-services, each owned by a small team in charge of one component: development, testing, monitoring, operations, automation, maintenance, documentation and delivery. These teams must still keep a global view of the whole service, so they move in the same direction and share knowledge.

    The knowledge I have acquired helps me work with this new software engineering paradigm. I keep learning, follow new technologies, improve and refactor. I am not afraid of change, and I adapt to the needs of the moment. I question myself, accept criticism, and communicate and share to try to do better. Experience lets you move faster, make better decisions, and be more creative and responsive, so you can go even further. For me, these skills matter more than a language or a framework, which is only a means. Yesterday I was developing in Java, today I work in Clojure, and tomorrow?

I train when I feel like it, in harmony with nature. For me, sport and IT share the same philosophy: train, optimize, iterate and rest to reach a personal best, and go further and faster.

    What I don’t like

    • Being restrained. I need to go at my own pace;
    • Repetitive tasks;
    • Anything that does not interest me;
    • Imposing a programming language and/or an operating system;
    • Boredom. I need challenges to stay motivated;
    • Too many meetings with too much speculation. I like to get straight to the point.
