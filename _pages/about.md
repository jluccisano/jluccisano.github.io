I am a kind of hybrid. I can work as a developer, architect, DevOps engineer, SRE or full stack engineer, and I do not really have one specific hat.
I do not consider myself an expert in every field, framework or language I have explored. My job is to find technical solutions to a functional need.
An engineer does not need to know everything. What matters is knowing how to find the right information to solve a problem or add a piece to the puzzle.
{: .text-justify}

Since 2023, I have been a Senior Principal Engineer at Semtech, where I design and operate a cloud-native, event-driven platform and take care of site reliability (SRE), which fits my philosophy. No two days are the same. I learn something new every day, which is why my work is my passion.

Cloud computing and SaaS platforms have made software more complex than ever. We are far from the CD-ROM era and the V model. Today we talk about scalability, high availability, big data and containers, in an agile model.
We need to develop, deploy and test fixes and new features, but we also need to scale and migrate quickly while keeping the service highly available.
Teams with a single narrow role would take too long to do this. So we split the service into micro-services, each owned by a small team in charge of one component: development, testing, monitoring, operations, automation, maintenance, documentation and delivery.
These teams must still keep a global view of the whole service, so they move in the same direction and share knowledge.
{: .text-justify}

The knowledge I have acquired helps me work with this new software engineering paradigm.
I keep learning, follow new technologies, improve and refactor. I am not afraid of change, and I adapt to the needs of the moment.
I question myself, accept criticism, and communicate and share to try to do better.
Experience lets you move faster, make better decisions, and be more creative and responsive, so you can go even further.
For me, these skills matter more than a language or a framework, which is only a means.
Yesterday I was developing in Java, today I work in Clojure, and tomorrow?
{: .text-justify}

## What I can do

- Build distributed, data-driven or event-driven architectures;
- Deploy a complete architecture in the cloud;
- Work with containerized applications;
- Think about scaling, migration, decoupling and vendor-agnostic solutions before coding;
- Develop micro-services and APIs;
- Quickly build an end-to-end proof of concept, with a GUI, a backend and more;
- Work with SQL and NoSQL databases, and with in-memory caches;
- Set up a continuous integration and delivery (CI/CD) system;
- Automate recurring tasks as much as possible;
- Set up alarms, metrics and dashboards to operate and monitor systems;
- Create an integration test environment;
- Work in English with teams in France, the United States, India and EMEA.

## What I like to do

- Refactor, migrate or, even better, start from scratch on a new architecture with everything to do;
- Have a goal and the autonomy to reach it;
- Stop everything and resume the next day after a good night's sleep or a good training session. It helps me unblock a situation;
- Build concrete projects for my own use. For several years, I have created projects and shared what I learned on this site. I built my own [sprinkler system](/automation/sprinkler/), my home alarm, my bikes, my wheels and my drone. See all my [projects](/projects/).
- My three pillars are my family, sport and IT. Sport clears my mind. I do a lot of endurance sports: triathlon, swimming, cycling, running and cross-country skiing. I train when I feel like it, in harmony with nature. For me, sport and IT share the same philosophy: train, optimize, iterate and rest to reach a personal best, and go further and faster.

## What I don't like

- Large teams with lots of processes;
- Being restrained. I need to go at my own pace;
- Repetitive tasks;
- Anything that does not interest me;
- Imposing a programming language and/or an operating system;
- Boredom. I need challenges to stay motivated;
- Too many meetings with too much speculation. I like to get straight to the point.
