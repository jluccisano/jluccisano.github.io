Why? 

Looking for a job has changed a lot, at least in IT. Before, you had to apply with a CV and a cover letter
to get an interview. Today, recruiters come directly to us. We are not going to complain about this situation, quite the contrary.
To avoid wasting time for both parties, I wanted to describe who I am, my vision and what I can do…
{: .text-justify}

I’m a kind of hybrid, I can be Developer, Architect, DevOps, SRE, Full Stack… I don’t really have a specific hat.
I don’t consider myself an expert in every field, framework or language I have explored. I am not a multifaceted person able to take actions
on every layer of software development. My job is to find technical solutions that rely on a functional requirement. The engineer’s job
is not to know everything but to know how to find relevant information in order to solve an issue or add a new piece to the “puzzle”.
{: .text-justify}

Since 2023, I have been a Senior Principal Engineer at Semtech, where I design and operate a cloud-native, event-driven platform and take care of site reliability (SRE), which fits my philosophy. No two days are the same. Every day I learn new things,
that’s why my job is my passion.  The age of cloud computing and SaaS platforms has made software ever more complex. We are far from the CD-ROM era,
following the V model. Today we talk about scalability, high availability, big data or containers, in an agile model.
We need to develop, deploy and test bugs and new features, but also to scale and migrate quickly to keep high availability.
Working with different teams, each with a specific role, would take too much time. To answer that, we split the service into micro-services with
small teams in charge of development and support of a specific component including testing, monitoring, operations, automation, maintenance, documentation and delivery…
But these teams must keep a global vision of the whole service, so they can move forward in the same way and share knowledge.
{: .text-justify}

The knowledge I have acquired helps me feel comfortable with this new software engineering paradigm.
Always learning, watching new technologies, improving, refactoring… Do not be afraid of change, and adapt to meet the needs of the moment.
Always questioning yourself, accepting criticism, communicating and sharing to try to do better.
Experience lets you go faster, make better decisions, and be more creative and responsive, so you can go even further.
For me, these are the skills a software engineer must have, rather than a language or a framework, which is only a means.
Yesterday I was developing in Java, today in Clojure, and tomorrow?
{: .text-justify}

# What can I do?

- Build distributed, data-driven or event-driven architectures;
- Deploy a complete architecture in the cloud;
- Work with containerized applications;
- Think about scaling, migration, decoupling and vendor-agnostic solutions before coding;
- Develop micro-services and APIs;
- Quickly develop an end-to-end PoC with GUI+Backend+…;
- Interact with SQL and NoSQL databases, and with in-memory caches;
- Set up a continuous integration and delivery system (CI/CD);
- Automate recurring tasks as much as possible;
- Set up alarms, metrics and dashboards to operate and monitor systems;
- Create an integration test environment;
- Work in an international context in English, with teams in France, the US, India and EMEA.

# What do I like to do?

- I like to refactor, migrate or, even better, start from scratch on a new architecture with everything to do
- I like having a goal and being given the autonomy to achieve it.
- My three pillars are my family, sport and IT. Sport frees my mind. 
  I practise a lot of endurance sports: triathlon, swimming, cycling, running, skate skiing... I practise when I want, 
  in harmony with nature. For me, sport and IT share the same philosophy: train, optimize,
  iterate and rest to meet a personal performance, go further and faster.
- I like to stop everything and resume the next day after a good night's sleep or a good training session, which helps me unblock a situation.
- I like to develop concrete projects for my personal use. So for several years I have created projects, and I posted all
my experiences on this site to share knowledge. I created my own sprinkler system and my home alarm.
  I built my bikes and wheels, my drone...

# What don’t I like?

- Large teams with lots of processes
- I don't like being restrained. I need to be able to go at my own pace
- I don’t tolerate repetitive tasks
- I avoid anything that does not interest me
- Imposing a programming language and/or an OS
- I can't stand boredom, I need challenges to keep my motivation
- Too many meetings with too much speculation. I like to get straight to the point.

# Qualities/Flaws according to the point of view

- I say what I think
- Very persistent
